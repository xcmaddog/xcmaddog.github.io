Flight, Learning, Optimization, and Wind (FLOW) Laboratory Mentored Projects

This page does not cover the paid work I did in the FLOW lab, but instead breifly covers the mentored projects I did under the FLOW Lab prior to becoming a research assistant.

Airfoils with XFOIL

The first project I did under the FLOW lab was designed to help familiarize myself with airfoils as well as one way to predict their performmance. I used XFOIL to analyze a handful of airfoils at various Reynolds numbers and angles of attack. I then compared the XFOIL results from one airfoil to wind tunnel data available online to see how accurate XFOIL's predictions were.

Airframe Analysis

The second project I did under the FLOW lab was to analyze an airframe (wing and tails). I used VortexLattice.jl, a Julia package developed by the FLOW lab to analyze the lifting surfaces using a vortex splines. I observed the effect of the number of panels used to model an eliptical wing. I also observed the effect of tail volume ratios on the stability derivatives of the aircraft.

Airframe Design

The third project I did under the Flow lab was to design a wing using computer optimization. Most of the parameters of the wing were fixed, but the optimizer had control over the chord lengths of each panel. The objective was to minimize drag while achieving a certain amount of lift at a given speed and altitude. The result (which was expected) was a wing with an elliptical planform. (Because the airfoil and twist were constant.)
